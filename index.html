Hypervelocity Acoustic Emission (AE) & Transverse Shock Wave Inversion

Distinguishing multi-kilometer-per-second hypervelocity penetrations from benign robotic berthing, astronaut EVA bumps, and internal thermal pops inside Nextel/Aerogel flexible shielding.

01

Rankine-Hugoniot Shock Hugoniot in Multi-Layer Aerogel

When a hypervelocity micrometeorite ($v_p \ge 7 \text{ km/s}$) strikes the sacrificial Nextel bumper, kinetic energy converts into an explosive plasma plume and extreme planar shock waves propagating into the compressed nanoporous aerogel core (${\rm SiO_2}$ density $\rho_0 \approx 0.08 \text{ g/cm}^3$):

$$U_s = c_0 + s \, u_p, \quad P_H = \rho_0 U_s u_p = \rho_0 \left(c_0 u_p + s \, u_p^2\right)$$

Because aerogel pore collapse occurs at gigapascal pressures ($P_H > 5 \text{ GPa}$), high-frequency acoustic modes ($f > 300\text{ kHz}$) are immediately excited with ultra-short wavelengths $\lambda = c_s / f < 1.5\text{ mm}$, yielding accelerations $a \sim 10^3 - 10^5 g$.

02

Lamb Wave Dispersion & $(a, \lambda)$ Discriminant

Flexible bladder walls behave as multi-layered thin plates supporting zero-order antisymmetric ($A_0$) and symmetric ($S_0$) Lamb wave modes. The structural acoustic acceleration spectrum measured by bonded PZT discs is given by:

$$a(\omega) = -\omega^2 \left[ \frac{\kappa \cdot E_{\rm kin}}{2\pi r \cdot \rho h c_p} \right] e^{-\alpha(\omega) r}, \quad \lambda(\omega) = \frac{2\pi c_p(\omega)}{\omega}$$

Mundane events (robotic arm grapple, docking port latching) introduce momentum transfer over milliseconds ($\tau \ge 10\text{ ms}$), restricting energy to low frequencies ($f < 2\text{ kHz}$, $\lambda > 30\text{ cm}$) and gentle accelerations ($a < 2 g$). The $(a, \lambda)$ phase-space cleanly separates the two regimes by 4 orders of magnitude.

03

Triangulation via Non-Iterative Hyperbolic Inversion

Four distributed Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT-5H) piezoelectric sensors record shock onset times $t_i$ via constant-fraction discriminators (CFD) at nanosecond resolution. The spatial coordinate of penetration $(x_0, y_0)$ satisfies the nonlinear system:

$$d_i = \sqrt{(x_i - x_0)^2 + (y_i - y_0)^2} = d_1 + c_g (t_i - t_1), \quad \forall i \in \{2, 3, 4\}$$

Applying Bancroft's closed-form pseudo-inverse eliminates iterative divergence, converging in under $4.2\,\mu\text{s}$ on flight-qualified radiation-hardened FPGA microcontrollers, pinpointing the puncture within $\pm 4.5\text{ mm}$.

04

Autonomous Drone Magnetic Vectoring & Patch Adhesion

Upon confirmation of penetration ($D(a, \lambda) > 0$), the internal atmospheric pressure delta triggers magnetic crawler drone dispatch. The drone tracks the acoustic coordinate $(x_0, y_0)$ using planar magnetic hull guide strips:

$$\dot{m}_{\rm leak} = C_d A_{\rm hole} P_{\rm hab} \sqrt{\frac{\gamma M}{R T} \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}}, \quad \tau_{\rm seal} \le \frac{V_{\rm hab} \Delta P_{\rm allow}}{\dot{m}_{\rm leak} R_{\rm spec} T}$$

Quick-curing polysiloxane elastomer patches are pneumatically stamped onto the multi-layer bladder puncture within 45 seconds, arresting pressure decline before emergency cabin evacuation thresholds are reached.
